Cincinnati Reds Hackathon 2025

Baseball Analytics

In this project, I participated in the Cincinnati Reds Hackathon 2025, where college teams were challenged to design data‑driven solutions for MLB front offices. The central problem focused on predicting player availability, specifically estimating plate appearances for batters and batters faced for pitchers during the 2024 season. This question of playing time is critical, as it directly impacts player valuation, roster construction, and long‑term team planning.

My approach centered on building a roster‑agnostic model that evaluated players in isolation, independent of positional competition. I explored historical performance data, injury history, age curves, and usage trends to identify the strongest predictors of playing time. The modeling process required careful feature engineering, regression analysis, and validation against prior seasons to ensure accuracy and interpretability. Beyond prediction, I emphasized uncovering the underlying drivers of playing time, such as durability, consistency, and team reliance.

The final submission presented a clear analytical pipeline, from data preprocessing to model selection, alongside insights into how front offices can leverage these predictions for contracts and roster efficiency. This project highlighted the importance of combining statistical rigor with practical baseball knowledge, demonstrating how analytics can enhance decision‑making in professional sports.
